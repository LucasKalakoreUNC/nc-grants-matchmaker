How this works

This tool checks federal and state grant listings every day and compares each one against your office's published strategic plan. It ranks them so you can spend your time on the grants most likely to be worth applying for.

Your weekly routine

  1. Choose your organization. First pick the type of organization, then your organization. The tool remembers your choice on this computer.
  2. Review what needs a decision. The first box at the top counts strong matches nobody in your office has decided on yet. Click it to see the list.
  3. Open a grant. Click the title. You will see why it fits your plan, how much it pays, the deadline, an estimate of staff time, and the documents you will need.
  4. Make a decision. Choose Pursue (we will apply), Watch (check back later), or Pass (not a fit). Add a short note so coworkers know why. Decisions are shared with your whole office.
  5. Share the list. Use "Download list" to get a spreadsheet for leadership or your weekly meeting.

What the match labels mean

Strong match Score of 70 or higher. Clearly supports one of your goals, and your organization is likely eligible. Review these first.
Good match Score of 55 to 69. Related to your goals. Worth a quick look.
Possible match Below 55. Loosely related. Usually safe to skip.

How grants are ranked

Every grant gets a score from 0 to 100 for your organization. The most important question is whether the grant actually fits your goals, so fit works as a gate: a grant with no real connection cannot score well, no matter how large or convenient it is.

Score = Fit x (0.6 + 0.4 x Practical / 100)

Fit with your strategic plan (0 to 100) combines two measures:

  • Key phrases (65%): looks for each goal's key phrases, such as "PFAS" or "highway safety", in the grant's title and description. A phrase in the title counts most. Common words like "water" or "health" on their own count very little. The grant page quotes the sentences that matched.
  • Word similarity (35%): compares all the words in the goal (its title, description and objectives) with all the words in the grant. Rare, specific words count more than common ones, and word endings are ignored. This catches grants that describe the same idea in different words. On its own it can only make a grant a "Possible match".

Practical factors (0 to 100) can lower a well-fitting grant's score by up to 40%:

Example: a grant with fit 80 and practical factors of 90 scores 80 x 0.96 = 77 (Strong). A $10 million grant your organization is eligible for, but with fit 15, scores at most 15.

Typical award: next to "Award up to", the page shows the median of recent awards nationwide under the same federal program (from USAspending.gov). If most awards are far below the maximum, it is marked in red.

Open any grant and choose "How this grant was scored" to see its numbers.

How staff time is estimated

Every application starts at 25 hours for the standard forms, narrative and budget. Time is added for: awards over $1 million (+20 hours) or over $5 million (+35), required matching funds (+20), construction or infrastructure work (+15), and required partners or letters of support (+10). Weeks assume about 15 staff hours per week. It is a rough guide to help plan, not a requirement.

Where the information comes from

Everything comes from public government sources. The tool saves what it downloads in its own database, so it only asks each source for new or changed information.
